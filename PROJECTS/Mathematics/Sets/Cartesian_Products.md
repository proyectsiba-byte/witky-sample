Cartesian Products

The Cartesian product of two sets "A" and "B" is the set of all ordered pairs "(a, b)" where "a ∈ A" and "b ∈ B".

It is represented by:

"A × B"

Example

Let:

"A = {1, 2}"

"B = {x, y}"

Then:

"A × B = {(1, x), (1, y), (2, x), (2, y)}"

Ordered Pairs

In an ordered pair "(a, b)", the order matters. In general:

"(a, b) ≠ (b, a)"

unless the two components are equal.

Cardinality

For finite sets "A" and "B":

"|A × B| = |A| × |B|"

If "A" has two elements and "B" has two elements, their Cartesian product has four ordered pairs.

If either set is empty, the Cartesian product is empty.
