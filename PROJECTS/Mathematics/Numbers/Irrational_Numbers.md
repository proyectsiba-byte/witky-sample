Irrational Numbers

Irrational numbers are real numbers that cannot be expressed as the quotient of two integers with a nonzero denominator.

Examples

- "√2"
- "π"
- "e"

The number "√2" is irrational because it cannot be expressed exactly as a fraction of two integers.

Decimal Representation

The decimal representation of an irrational number is non-terminating and non-repeating.

For example, the decimal expansion of "π" begins:

"3.1415926535..."

Its digits do not terminate or settle into a repeating pattern.

Rational vs. Irrational

- Rational numbers can be expressed as "a/b", where "a" and "b" are integers and "b ≠ 0".
- Irrational numbers cannot be expressed in that form.
- Both rational and irrational numbers belong to the real numbers.
